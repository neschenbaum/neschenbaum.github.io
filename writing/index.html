2026
Warum Fusionen die Preise erhöhen können – auch ohne Marktbeherrschung
Why mergers can raise prices, even without dominance
Swiss Economics blog, August 2026, with Samuel Rutz, Matthias Hafner, Michael Funk, and Seline Spillmann
The second part of a series on Switzerland's new SIEC test explains, using oligopoly theory, how a merger can raise prices without creating a dominant firm. It introduces concentration measures, diversion ratios, and upward pricing pressure as tools for assessing these effects, which still have to be weighed against efficiencies.

2026
Der SIEC-Test kommt in die Schweiz – Was ändert sich?
The SIEC test comes to Switzerland: what changes?
Swiss Economics blog, April 2026, with Samuel Rutz, Matthias Hafner, Michael Funk, and Seline Spillmann
Switzerland's revised Cartel Act replaces the dominance test in merger control with the EU's SIEC test. The post argues that this modernizes the method rather than tightening the law: it closes gap cases, considers efficiencies explicitly, and shifts the focus to a merger's effects on prices, quality, and innovation.

Revenue-management systems forecast demand and optimize prices but largely ignore how competitors will respond. The post argues that game theory, and compositional game theory in particular, can model these strategic interactions, and describes a pilot of competitor-aware dynamic pricing.

2024
Preissignale im Strommarkt stärken
Strengthening price signals in the electricity market
Die Volkswirtschaft, February 2024, with Urs Trinkner
Most Swiss households and small firms pay fixed annual tariffs, so wholesale price swings never reach them. Drawing on a study for SECO, the article shows that end customers do respond to prices, with elasticities of about −0.2 for households and −0.4 for firms, and proposes tariffs that pass on more of the price signal while keeping certainty.

Die Volkswirtschaft, July 2023, with Michael Funk and Samuel Rutz

Swiss Economics blog, June 2023
Reads the draft EU AI Act through a competition lens: its links to the DMA's gatekeeper concept and the separate obligations for foundation models, which do not fit the Act's risk-based scheme. 2022
AI and Its Effects on Competition
Swiss Economics blog, July 2022, with Nicolas Greber and Michael Funk
Sorts the competition risks of AI into three settings: interaction between competitors (algorithmic collusion and herding), third-party arrangements such as hub-and-spoke, and vertically integrated intermediaries that favour their own offers. The post recommends that firms cover how their algorithms behave in the market within their responsible-AI frameworks.

2020
Künstliche Intelligenz überlistet Wettbewerbsrecht
Artificial intelligence outsmarts competition law
Die Volkswirtschaft, December 2020
Self-learning pricing algorithms can learn to avoid price wars and sustain high prices without communicating, a form of tacit collusion that competition law, which hinges on agreement or intent, may not catch. The article argues for closing this gap, for example by treating reward-punishment pricing strategies as collusion or by letting authorities test pricing algorithms before they are deployed.

2024
Wirkung von Preissignalen und Regulierungen auf die Stromnachfrage
Effect of price signals and regulations on electricity demand
Study for the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO), Grundlagen für die Wirtschaftspolitik Nr. 47, with Urs Trinkner, Lilia Habibulina, Maida Sabotic, Romain de Luze, and Leah Meyer de Stadelhofen
Wholesale electricity demand barely responds to prices, but a 10% rise in end-customer prices cuts household demand by 1–3% and business demand by 2–7%. A survey of business customers finds that most passed higher electricity costs on to their own customers while working to cut consumption and invest in their own generation.
